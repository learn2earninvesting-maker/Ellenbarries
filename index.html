The story in one screen

What the business is, what has gone right, and the one question that matters next.

The central question
Can the new plants (both those built at customer sites and those selling to the open market), plus fuller use of existing plants and specialty gases, produce cash that keeps coming in year after year (durable operating cash flow)? That has to be weighed against execution, contracting and reporting watchpoints, and a share price that is already priced around or above the industry on some measures.

WORKINGWhat has gone right

  • Sales recovered from ₹205 cr in FY23 to ₹342 cr in FY26.
  • The share of sales left as operating profit (operating margin) jumped from 16.4% in FY23 to 34.0% in FY26.
  • Cash from day-to-day operations recovered to ₹133 cr in FY26, from just ₹4 cr in FY25.
  • Borrowings and net debt both came down in FY26.

WATCHWhat needs proof

  • Cash left after building plants (free cash flow) was negative in FY23, FY24, FY25 and FY26.
  • Sales growth slowed to 9.3% in FY26 from 16.0% in FY25.
  • Returns on money invested fell: ROCE 12.5% (from 16.9%), ROE 10.7% (from 16.9%).
  • Bank filings did not match the books in FY25 and FY26.
Bottom line from the report
The next leg of growth depends on commissioning and ramping new plants without tying up too much capital. The next annual audit and the pace of plant start-ups will be the key evidence.

  • ON-SITEPlant built at the customer's premises, gas piped directly. Contracts typically 15–20 years, often with take-or-pay terms (customer pays even if it uses less), guaranteed returns and costs passed through.
  • BULKLiquid gas delivered by tanker into the customer's storage tanks. Merchant contracts typically 3–7 years.

Why the mix matters
On-site contracts give long, predictable income; bulk and cylinder supply reach a much wider customer base. The two complement each other.

Audit & control scorecard

  • CLEANAudit opinion: unqualified (no reservations) for FY25 and FY26. No defaults or covenant breaches reported.
  • FLAGBank-return mismatch: quarterly statements filed with banks for working-capital loans above ₹5 cr did not match the books in both FY25 and FY26. A reporting-control issue, though no default was reported.
  • FLAGAudit-trail gaps: the system log of edits was switched on only from 21 Jun 2024 (FY25) and 29 Apr 2025 (FY26). Auditor could not verify payroll backups were kept in India from 1 Nov 2025.
  • CHECKProfit quality: FY26 profit includes ₹23.19 cr from revaluing land (dating from before 2016). The effective tax rate and partly-quantified Q4 one-offs also need watching when judging recurring profit.
  • CHECKPending claim: a disputed supplier claim of about ₹3.5 cr with no provision set aside.
  • DATESRepayments: term-loan instalments start Jan and Jun 2027; foreign-currency loans due by Jan and Mar 2027.
Overall
No defaults and no qualified audit opinion — but the bank-return mismatch, audit-trail gaps and unresolved supplier claim warrant monitoring. The next annual audit is the key evidence.

Risks alongside the growth case

Customer, cost, execution and safety risks named in the report, with the company's stated mitigations.

  • CUSTOMERConcentration: the FY26 annual report flags reliance on a limited number of customers and end-user industries. Mitigation: long-term contracts and diversification.
  • EXECUTIONMerchant ramp-up: new merchant plants take time to reach efficient use and are not pre-sold under advance contracts, so local customer wins and distribution matter more.
  • COSTPower and argon: electricity price or supply disruptions hit costs; argon prices swing with supply and demand. Mitigation: sourcing, inventory and customer-pricing measures where applicable.
  • SAFETYOperational safety: oxygen, hydrogen and acetylene handling, high pressure, extreme temperatures, and tanker or cylinder incidents. Mitigation: training, certified equipment, audits, emergency preparation.
  • PROJECTProject and supply continuity: turnkey project delays, logistics limits, storage constraints and supplier disruption can hurt delivery or project profit.
The risk that ties to the thesis
Because new merchant capacity is not pre-contracted, how fast Uluberia 2 and the North India plant fill up directly decides whether capex turns into cash.
